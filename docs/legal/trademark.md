# Trademark Policy

**Last updated:** August 2026

## Ownership

**V++** (spoken “V plus plus”) and the V++ logo are trademarks of the V++ open-source project. The name distinguishes our programming language, compiler, and ecosystem from unrelated projects.

“V++” is **not** a claim over the general concept of programming languages or the `++` suffix used elsewhere in computing.

## Permitted uses

You **may** (without asking):

- use the name **V++** in truthful statements (“built with V++”, “V++ user”, “compatible with V++ 0.5”);
- fork the software under the MIT License and state your fork is a derivative;
- link to the official site and downloads;
- use screenshots of the tooling in blog posts, talks, and reviews.

## Uses that require clarity

If you distribute a **modified** compiler or IDE extension:

- do **not** imply it is the official V++ release;
- use a distinct name or clearly label builds as “unofficial” or “patched”;
- do not use the official logo as your product icon without permission.

## Prohibited uses

You **may not**:

- register “V++” or confusingly similar marks for competing products;
- use the V++ logo in a way that suggests sponsorship or endorsement without permission;
- sell merchandise bearing the official logo without authorization.

## Logo

The logo files in the repository (`website/assets/`) are subject to this policy in addition to the MIT License. Copyright in the logo artwork remains with the project contributors.

## Questions

For trademark permissions beyond fair use, open a [GitHub Issue](https://github.com/shauryaR790/V-/issues) with the label `legal`.

## Note

This policy is not legal advice. It clarifies community expectations for an open-source project.
